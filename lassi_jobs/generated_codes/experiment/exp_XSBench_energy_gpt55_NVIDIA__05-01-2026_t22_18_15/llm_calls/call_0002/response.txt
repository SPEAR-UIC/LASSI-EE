## 1. Overall Algorithm and GPU Resource Utilization Profile### Algorithm- The code implements an event-based XSBench-style macroscopic cross-section lookup workload.- For each lookup:  1. A deterministic LCG RNG is fast-forwarded to generate a particle energy and material.  2. The material determines the number of nuclides to process.  3. For each nuclide in the material:     - A microscopic cross section is found by interpolation between two neighboring energy grid points.     - The microscopic XS vector is multiplied by the nuclide concentration and accumulated into a 5-element macroscopic XS vector.  4. A small verification value is generated from the index of the largest XS component.- The lookup path depends on `grid_type`:  - `UNIONIZED`: one binary search in the unionized energy grid, then indirect indexing into each nuclide grid.  - `NUCLIDE`: binary search inside each nuclide grid.  - `HASH`: hash-bin lookup followed by a bounded nuclide-grid search.### Resource utilization profile- The dominant workload is memory-latency and memory-bandwidth limited, not purely compute-bound.- There is substantial integer and double-precision arithmetic from:  - RNG fast-forwarding.  - Binary searches.  - Double-precision interpolation.  - Accumulation of 5 XS channels.- However, the main limiter is irregular memory access:  - Random particle energies produce random grid-search paths.  - Random material choices produce variable loop lengths.  - Nuclide grid accesses are indirect and scattered.  - Index-grid accesses are also scattered by energy and nuclide.- The workload has poor natural coalescing in the main lookup kernels.- Optimizations 4, 5, and especially 6 improve energy efficiency by grouping work by material and/or energy, reducing warp divergence and improving cache reuse.- The baseline and optimization 1 are likely inefficient on GPUs because neighboring threads in a warp generally perform unrelated lookups.### Energy-efficiency implications- Main energy loss sources:  - Uncoalesced global memory loads.  - Warp divergence from variable material sizes and binary-search paths.  - Heavy double-precision use.  - Repeated kernel launches and Thrust operations in some optimized variants.  - 32-thread block configurations limiting maximum occupancy on A100.- Best energy-efficiency opportunity:  - Group lookups by material and energy, as in optimization 6, if the sorting cost is amortized.  - Use lower precision where scientifically valid.  - Move small read-only material tables into constant memory.  - Increase block size for 32-thread kernels if register pressure allows.---## 2. CUDA Kernel Launch Configurations and A100 Occupancy Expectations### Baseline- Kernel:  - `xs_lookup_kernel_baseline`- Launch configuration:  - Threads per block: `256`  - Blocks: `ceil(in.lookups / 256.0)`- Occupancy on A100:  - 256 threads/block gives 8 warps/block.  - This can achieve high theoretical occupancy on A100 if register usage is moderate.  - Actual occupancy may be limited by register pressure from local arrays, inlined device functions, and double-precision temporaries.  - No shared memory limits occupancy.- Energy note:  - High occupancy may help hide latency, but warp divergence and scattered memory accesses still cause poor energy efficiency.### Optimization 1- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_1`- Launch configuration for both:  - Threads per block: `32`  - Blocks: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)`- Occupancy on A100:  - 32 threads/block gives 1 warp/block.  - A100 supports at most 32 resident blocks/SM, so this limits occupancy to at most 32 resident warps/SM, about 50% of the A100 64-warp maximum.  - Register pressure may reduce this further.- Energy note:  - Splitting sampling and lookup introduces extra global memory traffic for `p_energy_samples` and `mat_samples`.  - By itself, this likely increases energy consumption versus the baseline unless later grouping optimizations are used.### Optimization 2- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_2`, launched 12 times, once per material ID.- Launch configuration:  - `sampling_kernel`: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.  - Each material lookup kernel: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.- Occupancy on A100:  - Same 32-thread block occupancy ceiling: about 50% maximum theoretical occupancy.  - Each material kernel scans the entire lookup array; most threads return early.- Energy note:  - This reduces some material-loop divergence among active threads, but launches 12 full-grid kernels.  - Large energy overhead from repeatedly reading `mat_samples` and launching kernels where most threads do no useful work.### Optimization 3- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_3`, launched twice.- Launch configuration:  - `sampling_kernel`: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.  - Lookup kernels:    - `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block, with `is_fuel = 0`    - `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block, with `is_fuel = 1`- Occupancy on A100:  - Again capped at roughly 50% theoretical occupancy due to 1-warp blocks.- Energy note:  - Better than optimization 2 in launch count.  - Still scans the full lookup list twice.  - Reduces divergence between fuel and non-fuel work, but non-fuel materials still have variable nuclide counts.### Optimization 4- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_4`, launched once per material after sorting.- Launch configuration:  - `sampling_kernel`: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.  - For each material `m`:    - Threads per block: `32`    - Blocks: `ceil(n_lookups_per_material[m] / 32.0)`- Additional device work:  - 12 Thrust `count` operations.  - One Thrust `sort_by_key`.  - One Thrust `reduce`.- Occupancy on A100:  - Lookup kernels still have 32-thread blocks, so max theoretical occupancy is about 50%.  - Per-material kernels with small lookup counts may underfill the GPU.- Energy note:  - Sorting by material improves SIMD efficiency and removes most material-loop divergence within each material kernel.  - Sorting cost may be significant but can be worthwhile for large lookup counts.### Optimization 5- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_5`, launched twice after fuel/non-fuel partitioning.- Launch configuration:  - `sampling_kernel`: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.  - Fuel lookup kernel:    - Blocks: `ceil(n_fuel_lookups / 32.0)`    - Threads per block: `32`  - Non-fuel lookup kernel:    - Blocks: `ceil((in.lookups - n_fuel_lookups) / 32.0)`    - Threads per block: `32`- Additional device work:  - One Thrust `count`.  - One Thrust `partition`.  - One Thrust `reduce`.- Occupancy on A100:  - Same 32-thread block occupancy ceiling.  - Better useful occupancy than optimization 3 because the fuel and non-fuel kernels operate only on compacted regions.- Energy note:  - Likely more energy efficient than optimization 3 because it avoids scanning the entire lookup set twice.  - Still leaves divergence among non-fuel materials.### Optimization 6- Kernels:  - `sampling_kernel`  - `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_4`, launched once per material after material and energy sorting.- Launch configuration:  - `sampling_kernel`: `ceil(in.lookups / 32.0)` blocks, `32` threads/block.  - For each material `m`:    - Threads per block: `32`    - Blocks: `ceil(n_lookups_per_material[m] / 32.0)`- Additional device work:  - 12 Thrust `count` operations.  - One material `sort_by_key`.  - 12 per-material energy `sort_by_key` operations.  - One Thrust `reduce`.- Occupancy on A100:  - Lookup kernels remain capped at about 50% theoretical occupancy from 32-thread blocks.  - Sorting kernels are internally configured by Thrust and are likely to have good occupancy.- Energy note:  - This is the most cache-friendly and divergence-reducing version.  - Energy efficiency may be best for large lookup counts, despite the sorting overhead.  - For small lookup counts, sorting overhead may dominate.### General A100 occupancy recommendation- The 32-thread block size used in optimized kernels is suboptimal for maximum occupancy on A100.- Consider 128 or 256 threads/block for lookup kernels if register pressure allows.- Material/energy grouping can still be preserved with larger blocks.- Increasing block size may improve occupancy and energy efficiency by hiding memory latency better, but must be validated against register pressure and cache behavior.---## 3. Global Memory Access Patterns### `sampling_kernel`- Reads:  - No large global input arrays except `Inputs` and `SimulationData` metadata passed by value.- Writes:  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`- Access pattern:  - Consecutive thread → consecutive address.  - Writes are coalesced.- Energy profile:  - Efficient global stores.  - Main cost is RNG arithmetic, especially `fast_forward_LCG`.### `xs_lookup_kernel_baseline`- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`  - Consecutive and coalesced.- Reads:  - `num_nucs[mat]`: small, random by material.  - `mats[mat * max_num_nucs + j]`: material-dependent, partly reused across threads only if material matches.  - `concs[mat * max_num_nucs + j]`: same pattern as `mats`.  - `unionized_energy_array`: binary search over globally sorted energies; accesses are scattered across the array.  - `index_grid[idx * n_isotopes + nuc]`: scattered by `idx` and `nuc`.  - `nuclide_grid[nuc * n_gridpoints + index]`: scattered and indirect.- Access pattern:  - Mostly scattered in the main lookup.  - Poor coalescing for energy-grid, index-grid, and nuclide-grid loads.- Energy profile:  - High memory-transaction overhead and poor cache locality.  - Neighboring lanes often fetch unrelated cache lines.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_1`- Reads:  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - Main lookup arrays: same scattered pattern as baseline.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: coalesced.- Access pattern:  - Sampling data access is coalesced.  - Cross-section lookup remains scattered.- Energy profile:  - Additional global memory traffic versus baseline.  - No major locality benefit unless paired with sorting/grouping.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_2`- Reads:  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - For active threads only, `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced among active lanes but sparsely utilized.  - Lookup arrays: scattered.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: address is based on original `i`; active writes are sparse but still ordered by thread index.- Access pattern:  - The material filter causes many lanes to return early.  - Active lanes within a warp may be sparse, causing underutilized memory transactions.- Energy profile:  - Poor energy efficiency due to 12 full-array scans.  - Reduces some intra-kernel material divergence but wastes work on inactive threads.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_3`- Reads:  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced for active threads.  - Lookup arrays: scattered.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: sparse for each of the two kernels.- Access pattern:  - Better than optimization 2 because only two full scans are used.  - Still has sparse active lanes.- Energy profile:  - Fuel/non-fuel split reduces extreme divergence between fuel and other materials.  - Non-fuel material variability remains.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_4`- Reads:  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced within material-sorted ranges.  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - `mats` and `concs`: more uniform because all threads in a launch target the same material.  - `nuclide_grid` and `index_grid`: still scattered by energy, unless energies happen to be close.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: coalesced within the sorted contiguous segment.- Access pattern:  - Much better control-flow locality by material.  - Memory accesses to nuclide data remain scattered unless energy locality is also improved.- Energy profile:  - Better warp efficiency than baseline, optimization 1, 2, or 3.  - Sorting overhead must be amortized.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_5`- Reads:  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced within fuel/non-fuel partitioned segments.  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - Lookup arrays: scattered.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: coalesced within each compacted segment.- Access pattern:  - Fuel region is homogeneous and reduces severe divergence.  - Non-fuel region remains mixed and still divergent.- Energy profile:  - Lower overhead than full material sorting.  - Good compromise if full sort cost is too high.### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_6`- Reads:  - `GSD.p_energy_samples[i]`: coalesced and sorted by energy within material.  - `GSD.mat_samples[i]`: coalesced.  - `unionized_energy_array`, `index_grid`, and `nuclide_grid`: improved locality because adjacent threads have nearby energies and the same material.- Writes:  - `GSD.verification[i]`: coalesced within sorted segments.- Access pattern:  - Best locality among the variants.  - Consecutive threads are more likely to search similar grid regions and access nearby nuclide grid points.- Energy profile:  - Likely the best lookup-kernel energy efficiency.  - Additional sorting energy is the main tradeoff.### Thrust operations- `thrust::reduce`:  - Reads `GSD.verification` sequentially.  - Access is coalesced and bandwidth efficient.- `thrust::count`:  - Sequential scan of `GSD.mat_samples`.  - Coalesced.  - Repeated 12 times in optimizations 4 and 6.- `thrust::sort_by_key` and `thrust::partition`:  - Internally memory-intensive.  - Likely coalesced but high bandwidth and temporary-storage demand.  - Energy cost can be significant.---## 4. Shared Memory Usage### Current shared memory usage- No explicit `__shared__` memory is used in any CUDA kernel.- There are no `extern __shared__` allocations.- No `__syncthreads()` calls are present.### Local memory/register usage- Per-thread local arrays include:  - `macro_xs_vector[5]`  - `xs_vector[5]`  - `dist[12]` inside `pick_mat`- These may reside in registers if the compiler can keep them there.- If register pressure is high, spills to local memory may occur, increasing global memory traffic and energy consumption.### Potential shared/constant memory opportunities- Good candidates for constant memory:  - `num_nucs[12]`  - Small material metadata.  - `dist[12]` in `pick_mat`- Possible shared-memory candidates:  - Per-material `mats` and `concs` for the active material in material-specific kernels.  - This is most attractive in optimizations 4 and 6, where all threads in a kernel operate on the same material.- Caveat:  - Fuel material has up to 321 nuclides, so caching all fuel `mats` and `concs` per block is feasible but not free.  - Shared-memory staging would require cooperation and synchronization, which currently does not exist.- Energy opportunity:  - Moving small, repeatedly read, read-only data to constant memory can reduce global traffic and improve cache efficiency.  - Shared-memory staging of material composition may reduce redundant global loads in material-sorted kernels.---## 5. Warp Divergence### Major divergence sources- Random material selection gives different `num_nucs[mat]` loop counts per thread.- Fuel material has far more nuclides than other materials.- Binary searches take different paths for different energies.- Grid-type branches are uniform across the grid but still present.- Edge-case branches in interpolation differ by thread.- Material-filtering kernels intentionally cause many threads to return early.### Branches inside kernels and device functions#### `xs_lookup_kernel_baseline`- `if (i >= in.lookups) return;`  - Divergence only in the final partial block.- Verification max loop:  - `if (macro_xs_vector[j] > max)`  - Small branch, per thread.#### `sampling_kernel`- `if (i >= in.lookups) return;`  - Divergence only in the final partial block.- Calls `pick_mat`, which contains divergent branches.#### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_1`- `if (i >= in.lookups) return;`- Verification max branch:  - `if (macro_xs_vector[j] > max)`#### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_2`- `if (i >= in.lookups) return;`- `if (mat != m) return;`  - Significant divergence and inactive-lane waste.- Verification max branch.#### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_3`- `if (i >= in.lookups) return;`- Main fuel/non-fuel condition:  - `if (((is_fuel == 1) && (mat == 0)) || ((is_fuel == 0) && (mat != 0)))`  - Significant divergence before partitioning.- Verification max branch.#### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_4`- `if (i >= n_lookups) return;`- `if (mat != m) return;`  - Mostly redundant after sort, but still present.- Verification max branch.#### `xs_lookup_kernel_optimization_5`- `if (i >= n_lookups) return;`- Verification max branch.- No explicit material filter inside this kernel.#### `calculate_macro_xs`- `if (grid_type == UNIONIZED) ... else if (grid_type == HASH) ...`  - Usually uniform across all threads, so not major warp divergence.- Loop:  - `for (int j = 0; j < num_nucs[mat]; j++)`  - Major divergence when materials differ within a warp.  - Reduced by material grouping in optimizations 4 and 6.- Inner accumulation loop:  - `for (int k = 0; k < 5; k++)`  - Uniform.#### `calculate_micro_xs`- Grid-type branch:  - `if (grid_type == NUCLIDE) ... else if (grid_type == UNIONIZED) ... else`  - Uniform if one grid type is used globally.- Edge branches:  - `if (idx == n_gridpoints - 1)`  - `if (index_data[...] == n_gridpoints - 1)`  - Hash-grid edge branches:    - `if (idx == hash_bins - 1)`    - `if (p_energy <= e_low)`    - `else if (p_energy >= e_high)`    - `else`  - These are per-thread and may diverge.#### `grid_search` and `grid_search_nuclide`- Loop:  - `while (length > 1)`- Branch:  - `if (A[examinationPoint] > quarry)`  - Binary-search paths diverge by energy.  - Energy sorting in optimization 6 reduces this divergence.#### `pick_mat`- Outer loop:  - `for (int i = 0; i < 12; i++)`- Inner loop:  - `for (int j = i; j > 0; j--)`- Branch:  - `if (roll < running) return i;`- Divergence depends on random material selection.- Also contributes extra per-thread arithmetic.#### `fast_forward_LCG`- Loop:  - `while (n > 0)`- Branch:  - `if (n & 1)`- Since `n = 2*i`, different threads have different bit patterns.- This creates control-flow divergence during sampling.---## 6. Data Types and Double-Precision Replacement Opportunities### Current double-precision dataThe following are double-precision:- `NuclideGridPoint.energy`- `NuclideGridPoint.total_xs`- `NuclideGridPoint.elastic_xs`- `NuclideGridPoint.absorbtion_xs`- `NuclideGridPoint.fission_xs`- `NuclideGridPoint.nu_fission_xs`- `concs`- `unionized_energy_array`- `p_energy_samples`- `p_energy`- `macro_xs_vector[5]`- `xs_vector[5]`- Interpolation factor `f`- Material distribution values in `pick_mat`- RNG return values from `LCG_random_double`### Replaceable with float if accuracy permitsPotentially replaceable with single precision:- Particle energy samples.- Nuclide grid energies.- Cross-section values.- Concentrations.- Interpolation arithmetic.- `macro_xs_vector` and `xs_vector`.- Material probability distribution in `pick_mat`.- Unionized energy grid.### Energy-efficiency impact- Replacing doubles with floats would:  - Reduce memory footprint for major arrays.  - Improve cache residency.  - Reduce memory bandwidth demand.  - Reduce energy per arithmetic operation.  - Potentially increase throughput significantly.- On A100, FP64 throughput is strong compared with many GPUs, but FP64 still has higher energy cost and larger memory traffic than FP32.### Correctness caution- Changing double to float will likely change the verification checksum.- If bitwise reproducibility is required, these replacements cannot be made without updating validation criteria.- If scientific tolerance allows, mixed precision may be a strong energy optimization:  - Store grid data in float.  - Accumulate macro XS in float or possibly double.  - Keep RNG indexing deterministic.---## 7. Primary Energy-Efficiency Opportunities### Highest-impact opportunities1. Prefer material and energy grouping for lookup kernels.   - Optimization 6 gives the best locality and least divergence.   - Optimization 5 may be a lower-overhead compromise.2. Increase optimized kernel block size.   - 32-thread blocks limit A100 occupancy to about 50%.   - Try 128 or 256 threads/block for lookup kernels if register pressure permits.3. Reduce double precision where acceptable.   - Storing grid data and concentrations as float could nearly halve memory traffic.   - Mixed precision may preserve much of the accuracy while reducing energy.4. Use constant memory for small read-only data.   - `num_nucs`, material distributions, and possibly small material metadata are strong candidates.5. Improve read-only cache behavior.   - Use read-only-qualified paths where possible.   - Material-sorted kernels make `mats` and `concs` much more cache-friendly.6. Avoid repeated full-array scans.   - Optimization 2 is especially energy-inefficient because it launches 12 full-grid material filters.   - Compaction or prefix-sum based work queues are more energy efficient.7. Reduce sorting overhead when possible.   - Full sorting is expensive.   - If lookups are reused over multiple physics steps, sorting cost can be amortized.   - If only fuel/non-fuel imbalance matters, partitioning may be more efficient than full sorting.8. Monitor register pressure.   - Local arrays and inlined functions may inflate register usage.   - Excessive register pressure reduces occupancy or causes spills, both hurting energy efficiency.9. Consider data layout alternatives.   - The current `NuclideGridPoint` AoS layout is reasonable when all fields are consumed together by one thread.   - But because neighboring threads access unrelated grid points, memory coalescing is poor.   - Energy-sorted access is likely more beneficial than changing AoS to SoA unless only a subset of fields is needed.
